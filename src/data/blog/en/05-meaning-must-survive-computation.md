People naturally distinguish things that software often compresses into states, flags and fields.

A claim is not truth. An observation is not the thing observed. A prediction is not an event. A hypothesis is not the current world. Permission is not authority. Authority is not a decision. A decision is not execution. Execution is not an effect.

These distinctions determine how information may be used. A high-risk prediction may inform a decision without acquiring the authority of that decision. A hypothesis can be explored without changing the reference world. An executed action may produce an effect different from the one expected.

FormaSL makes these distinctions explicit before choosing how to store or execute them.

That allows disagreement, context, time, authority, uncertainty and history to remain computable instead of collapsing into a single apparently true state.

**Modeling meaning begins by preserving what must not be confused.**
