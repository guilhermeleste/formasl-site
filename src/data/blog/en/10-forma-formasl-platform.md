The architecture becomes simpler when three things are kept clearly separate.

**Forma is the company.**

It develops Semantic Computing, maintains FormaSL and builds the ecosystem required to turn that architecture into software infrastructure.

**FormaSL is the open-source language.**

It lets organizations declare worlds, relations and rules in a computable form that is independent from the technologies that realize them.

**Forma Platform is the commercial product.**

It connects FormaSL contracts to databases, services, models, agents and infrastructure and provides the mechanisms needed to operate those contracts in production.

The division is deliberate.

An organization’s meaning should remain the organization’s property. The open language allows contracts to be read, preserved and realized outside a proprietary service. Companies and third parties can build tools, libraries and alternative realizations.

Forma monetizes the operational layer.

**We do not sell the right to define meaning. We sell the ability to put computable meaning into production.**

That places Forma in a different position from traditional clouds.

A cloud provides primitives: compute, storage, databases, queues, containers, identity, networking, observability and models. The developer selects those pieces and composes a backend.

Forma adds a level of abstraction above them.

The developer starts from the system to be built: concepts, relations, rules, context and contracts. The platform turns part of that definition into operational infrastructure.

This reduces the amount of composition that must be rebuilt for every application without removing access to lower layers.

Traditional code remains available. External databases remain available. Existing clouds remain available. An application can use Forma in a single domain or adopt the platform much more broadly.

The architecture is composable by design.

The goal is not to replace all existing software. It is to introduce a layer that is currently missing.

That layer creates an important commercial model: organizations retain control over what differentiates them and externalize the generic infrastructure required to make that meaning computable, governable and reusable.

**The organization owns its world. Forma provides the infrastructure to make it work.**

That proposition becomes most valuable in sectors where meaning crosses many systems and disagreement has real consequences.
