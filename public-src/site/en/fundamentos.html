The world, before the software.

Forma starts from a simple premise: different agents know and describe reality from their own positions. Its language represents these positions and supports computation without erasing them.

01 / Semantic ComputingFORMA

Meaning belongs in the program.

An application declares relations, rules, claims and operations as semantic structures. Infrastructure realizes these declarations; it does not redefine their meaning for convenience.

The same information may be stored, analyzed or displayed in different ways. Interpretable results depend on retaining the concepts and conditions that give them meaning.

02 / World modelFORMA

Situated agents. A shared world.

A person, organization or computational agent observes and makes statements within a particular context, with particular information and responsibilities. The world model represents these perspectives without assuming any one of them exhausts reality.

Two organizations may describe the same delivery while recording different events. The program can relate their information without losing the origin of each position.

03 / Attributable act of assertionFORMA

What is a Claim?

A Claim represents an identifiable assertion with a speaker, content and relevant conditions of interpretation. One person may state that a shipment arrived while another has not confirmed receipt.

Recording a Claim does not establish that the described event occurred. Observations, assessments and decisions are separate acts.

04 / Logical metagraphFORMA

Relations have participants and roles.

In FormaSL, a relation may bring together multiple participants within one occurrence. An assignment can involve a person, an artifact, a project and an environment, each in a defined role.

The metagraph organizes these typed relations and allows references to them. Diagrams and physical structures are possible representations, not the source of an occurrence’s logical identity.

05 / Semantic continuityFORMA

Results must remain interpretable.

A query, analysis or transformation may produce new information. Its result needs to retain, directly or through reconstructible references, the conditions needed for interpretation.

That does not require copying all data at each step. It requires accounting for what was preserved, derived, transformed, restricted or explicitly discarded.
